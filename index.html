Given one motion-blurred image, PickMoment answers three kinds of queries with the same network: (a) the sharp frame at the exposure center, (b) an $N$-frame sharp video, and (c) the sharp frame at any moment inside the exposure.

Abstract

Motion blur arises from the temporal integration of a continuous sharp signal over a finite exposure window, yet existing learning-based methods sidestep this physical model and predict only the sharp signal itself: most single-image deblurring methods recover a single frame at the exposure center, while blur-to-video methods predict a fixed set of frames. We introduce PickMoment, a continuous-time reformulation that directly learns the interval-mean blur over arbitrary sub-intervals of the exposure with a single deterministic model. Drawing an analogy to MeanFlow's average-velocity formulation, we train the model with three supervisions derived from the blur integral: an empirical reconstruction loss from available subframes, an additivity loss that enforces self-consistency across overlapping sub-intervals, and a sharp-frame loss anchored at the zero-interval limit. A single trained model unifies single-image deblurring, blur-to-video generation, and continuous-time pick-a-moment recovery as different queries to the same network, with no separate training for each task. Our PickMoment achieves state-of-the-art performance among generative-prior-based deblurring methods on GoPro and HIDE while competitive against restoration-based methods on RealBlur, and the highest per-frame fidelity on GoPro-7 blur-to-video, all with one forward pass per output frame and no iterative sampling.

Key Idea: One Function, Many Queries

A motion-blurred photo is the average of the sharp scene over the exposure. PickMoment learns this averaging for every sub-interval, not only for the full exposure:

$$ B(s,t) \;=\; \frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t} S(u)\,\mathrm{d}u $$

for any $0 \le s < t \le 1$ in normalized exposure time. The observed blurry image is $B(0,1)$, and as the interval shrinks, $B(s,t)$ converges to the sharp frame $S(\tau)$ at that instant. Every task becomes a query to the same network:

  Single-image deblurring
$S(\tfrac12)$
A zero-width query at the exposure center. One forward pass.
  Blur-to-video
$\{S(\tau_i)\}_{i=0}^{N-1},\ \tau_i = \tfrac{i}{N-1}$
$N$ zero-width queries on the same input: one forward pass per output frame, issued as a single batch.
  Pick-a-moment
$S(\tau)$ or $B(s,t)$
Any instant inside the exposure, or the blur of any sub-exposure. The other two tasks are special cases of this one.
Relation to MeanFlow
MeanFlow learns the time-average of an instantaneous velocity, so that one network evaluation replaces integration over an interval. PickMoment applies the same interval-averaging operator to a different quantity: sharp frames over exposure time. What carries over is the structure of an interval mean, namely additivity across sub-intervals and the zero-width limit. The stochastic noise-to-data sampling of MeanFlow does not: PickMoment builds on a pretrained generative prior but runs deterministically, so it does not retain the sampling-based generative capability. Because the interval mean of sharp frames is the observation itself, it can also be supervised directly.

Method

The network $f_\theta(B(0,1), s, t)$ predicts $B(s,t)$ in the latent space of a frozen VAE. It is fine-tuned from a pretrained image generative transformer (FLUX.2-klein), with the interval $(s,t)$ injected in place of the text conditioning. An image model thus solves a video task, with no video pretraining. Training uses three supervisions derived from the blur integral:

Reconstruction $\mathcal{L}_\text{recon}$
Supervises $B(s,t)$ on sampled sub-intervals with the empirical mean of the available subframes.
Additivity $\mathcal{L}_\text{add}$
For every split point $m$, an interval mean is the length-weighted average of its two parts: \[ \begin{aligned} B(s,t) = {} & \tfrac{m-s}{t-s}\,B(s,m) \\ & + \tfrac{t-m}{t-s}\,B(m,t). \end{aligned} \] Enforcing it ties all sub-interval predictions on one input into a single consistent trajectory.
Sharp frame $\mathcal{L}_\text{sharp}$
Anchors zero-width queries $B(\tau,\tau)$ to the sharp frame $S(\tau)$, supervised in pixel space after decoding.

Trade-off
PickMoment leads on per-frame fidelity (PSNRp, SSIMp, LPIPSp). Blur2Vid leads on FVD and EPE, the two metrics that judge the sequence as a whole. This follows from the prior: Blur2Vid fine-tunes a video diffusion model (CogVideoX-2B) and samples the whole sequence jointly, while PickMoment uses an image-only prior with no cross-frame attention and answers each query independently. Against every other baseline, PickMoment's FVD and EPE are far ahead.

What Does the Interval-Mean Formulation Add?

Same backbone, different target. With backbone, data and budget fixed, and only a latent reconstruction loss (no sharp-frame or perceptual loss), predicting the interval mean beats predicting a single sharp frame by 0.73 dB and a fixed set of $N$ frames by 0.69 dB in deblurring PSNR (0.62 / 0.52 dB in PSNRp).

Sharp-frame loss alone. Training with $\mathcal{L}_\text{sharp}$ only, so that no interval-mean supervision remains, nearly matches the full objective on deblurring but falls well behind on the video metrics. The formulation's contribution is temporal.
